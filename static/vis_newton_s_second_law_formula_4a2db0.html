Newton's Second Law of Motion

Newton's second law quantifies the relationship between net force, mass, and acceleration (F = m · a).

$$F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad a = \frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m}$$

Variable Breakdown & Units

F Net Force Newtons (N)
Vector sum of external forces
m Mass Kilograms (kg)
Quantitative measure of inertia
a Acceleration m/s²
Rate of change of velocity

Derivation & Step-by-Step Logic

State Law
$$F = \frac{dp}{dt}$$
Time rate of change of momentum.
Constant Mass
$$F = m \cdot a$$
Assuming constant mass.
Practical Worked Example:
For mass m = 10 kg and force F = 50 N: a = F / m = 50 / 10 = 5 m/s².
